CONTEXT & OWNERSHIP

The problem and my role.

01 / THE CHALLENGE

Why the work mattered

Installation and service timing could change in the customer-facing system without being reflected in the material forecast used by MRP. When the two timelines diverged, site materials risked being planned for the wrong delivery window.

02 / MY CONTRIBUTION

What I owned

I built the structured reconciliation dataset and date-comparison logic, created site-level exception visibility, and coordinated with MRP controllers to resolve mismatches.

SOLUTION DESIGN

How I approached it.

An end-to-end look at the planning logic, stakeholder workflow and delivery approach.

01

Bring milestones together

Structured Salesforce service and installation-kit milestone dates in an Oracle reporting table for repeatable comparison.

02

Align at the site level

Matched milestones to the corresponding forecast demand dates in MRP rather than relying on broad aggregate demand views.

03

Identify mismatched timing

Flagged sites with meaningful date differences so reviewers could focus on the highest-risk planning records.

04

Close the planning loop

Worked with MRP controllers and planning stakeholders to review the exceptions and correct forecast inputs where appropriate.

BUSINESS OUTCOMES

What changed.

Qualitative results are described without publishing unapproved internal performance figures.

  • Made date misalignment visible before it emerged as a last-minute material shortage.
  • Established a structured feedback loop between installation scheduling and materials planning.
  • Reduced dependence on one-off manual comparisons of milestone and forecast timing.
