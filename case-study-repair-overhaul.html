CONTEXT & OWNERSHIP

The problem and my role.

01 / THE CHALLENGE

Why the work mattered

Repair-and-overhaul teams faced recurring material availability issues and line-down risk. Production control, buyers, service and operations needed a consistent view of at-risk parts and follow-up responsibilities.

02 / MY CONTRIBUTION

What I owned

I managed material planning and weekly shortage reporting for the repair-and-overhaul organization and coordinated the resulting follow-ups across execution teams.

SOLUTION DESIGN

How I approached it.

An end-to-end look at the planning logic, stakeholder workflow and delivery approach.

01

Consolidate shortage signals

Produced a recurring material-risk view based on production needs, available stock and incoming supply.

02

Rank operational constraints

Identified parts that could interrupt repair-and-overhaul work and prioritized follow-up before the expected need dates.

03

Coordinate across teams

Shared the shortage view with production control, production, service and buyers to identify recovery actions.

04

Maintain an action cadence

Used recurring weekly reviews to track outstanding items, timing changes and ownership until the risks were addressed.

BUSINESS OUTCOMES

What changed.

Qualitative results are described without publishing unapproved internal performance figures.

  • Supported a substantial observed reduction in recurring line-down situations.
  • Created shared visibility so multiple teams could act from the same shortage priorities.
  • Shifted conversations from reactive firefighting toward earlier material-risk identification.
