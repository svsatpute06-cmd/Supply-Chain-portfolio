CONTEXT & OWNERSHIP

The problem and my role.

01 / THE CHALLENGE

Why the work mattered

Buyers and planners needed one reliable way to see components falling below safety-stock coverage, material building above policy, and item attributes that could prevent replenishment decisions. Fragmented reviews made ownership and exceptions harder to manage.

02 / MY CONTRIBUTION

What I owned

I designed the exception logic, developed the custom SQL dataset, built the dashboard, configured role-based reporting, and owned a recurring data-quality review with stakeholders.

SOLUTION DESIGN

How I approached it.

An end-to-end look at the planning logic, stakeholder workflow and delivery approach.

01

Model ASCP supply and demand

Used Oracle ASCP planning data, including MSC_ORDERS_V, to make new demand and planning-attribute changes visible in the same reporting flow.

02

Prioritize both sides of inventory risk

Highlighted parts below a three-week coverage threshold alongside potential excess stock above policy, helping buyers distinguish shortages from over-ordering.

03

Secure stakeholder access

Applied row-level security so executives, managers, planners and buyers could use appropriate views of the same reporting framework.

04

Make data ownership operational

Established a weekly review of missing buyer assignments, safety-stock settings, sourcing rules, lead times and related master data.

BUSINESS OUTCOMES

What changed.

Qualitative results are described without publishing unapproved internal performance figures.

  • Created a common view of inventory exceptions for leadership and execution teams.
  • Turned recurring item setup problems into a prioritized review rather than scattered manual checks.
  • Helped distinguish genuine safety-stock gaps from items intentionally managed without safety-stock policies.
