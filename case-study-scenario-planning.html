CONTEXT & OWNERSHIP

The problem and my role.

01 / THE CHALLENGE

Why the work mattered

Revenue and repair-and-overhaul planners needed to model different product variants, build volumes and dates without manually recreating an entire materials plan. A useful answer required more than a static parts list: it needed projected availability over time.

02 / MY CONTRIBUTION

What I owned

I designed the scenario input and material-requirements logic, connected the file-refresh workflow, and delivered the time-phased component risk views for weekly planner and buyer decisions.

SOLUTION DESIGN

How I approached it.

An end-to-end look at the planning logic, stakeholder workflow and delivery approach.

01

Translate builds into demand

Allowed planners to enter variant quantities and required dates, then exploded multi-level BOMs down to purchased components.

02

Automate scenario ingestion

Used an S3-based upload process with Glue and Athena so refreshed scenario files could feed an analytics dataset.

03

Net demand against scheduled supply

Compared dated buy-part requirements with Oracle opening inventory and incoming purchase-order quantities.

04

Surface the required buyer action

Calculated projected running on-hand balances and identified potential purchase-order pull-ins, push-outs and supply gaps.

BUSINESS OUTCOMES

What changed.

Qualitative results are described without publishing unapproved internal performance figures.

  • Enabled side-by-side evaluation of proposed builds against material feasibility.
  • Gave planners a time-phased picture of what would be needed, not just total component quantities.
  • Supported more specific weekly conversations with buyers about timing and supply recovery.
